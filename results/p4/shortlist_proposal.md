## 2. Justification and robustness

- **Headline-rule strata.** All three finalists have the same sign at n = 160 on the full set, on E_AO and on E_VV
  (the dev analogue of E_TT). The gain is larger on E_VV (+36 to +42%) than on E_AO (+22 to +27%).
- **On-array target rows only.** The gain persists when off-array query rows are removed (+35 to +40% at n = 160).
  The effect is therefore not the yeast off-array artefact (D5).
- **Off-array share stays near base.** Under the randomised rule the finalists pick off-array genes at close to the
  base rate:

  | arm | n = 160 | n = 660 | base |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | H | 0.17 | 0.18 | 0.19 |
  | C | 0.19 | 0.19 | 0.19 |
  | F_r16 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.19 |

  M picks almost no off-array genes (0.015 at n = 160, 0.004 at n = 660). That follows from its spec ("no GI column ⇒
  never picked") and is itself a yeast artefact (every HAP1 gene is in the library). This is a further reason, beyond
  the rule, to prefer C over M as the C/M finalist.
- **The value comes from iteration.** H-static gains +13.4% and F-static −6.5%, against +32.7% and +28.0% for their
  adaptive versions. R1 (Billmann-like round-robin) equals random (+0.7%).
- **The gain holds up to n = 660.** Relative Δ at n = 660 is +28.2% for H, +22.6% for C and +27.1% for F_r16 (summary.md).
- **Ranking within the top group is not resolved.** The differences between H, C, F_r16 and the M variants (+28 to
  +33%) are within noise:
  - Trajectory SD at n = 160 is 0.0017–0.0037.
  - The shared-weight replicates' V_Q is about 1.4e-5 per split.
  - The M λ screen shows the winner's curse directly. λ = 0.03 led at +33.6% on trajectory 0 and fell to +28.5% once
    trajectories 1–2 were added.

  The order H > C > F is the rule's output, not evidence that one finalist beats another.

Target: SE(Δ̂(160)) ≤ g/3.5, where g = r210 − r160 = 0.0025 (frozen-P* dev random curves, mean of splits). That gives
a target SE of 0.0007.

|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1.39e-5 | 0.0022 | 0.0018 |
| C | 1.10e-5 | 0.0017 | 0.0018 |
| F_r16 | 1.48e-5 | 0.0019 | 0.0018 |

- V_Q alone is about 25× the target SE² (4.9e-7), so no R reaches the target. Under the frozen rule the **Q2 claims
  stay "bounds only"**, as the P1 pilot found.
- SE barely depends on R:

  | R_s (R_r = 2·R_s) | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SE(Δ̂(160)), H | 0.0039 | 0.0038 | 0.0038 | 0.0037 |

**Proposal: R_s = 20 and R_r = 40.**
- SE is within 2% of the R = 40 value.
- Under "R may only go up from the pilot": the frozen-rule pilot gave bounds only, with no finite R, and its
  shared-weight sensitivity suggested R_s ≈ 35–39.
- If you treat 39 as the provisional R, then R_s = 39 and R_r = 78. That costs about twice the compute and buys
  < 1% SE.

Projected final cost. Dev trajectories took 240–290 s at 2 threads; I assume about 300 s on the larger final pool and
T. For finalists, random, R1 and the 2 static counterparts:

|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 3·20 + 40 + 20 + 2·20 = 160 | ≈ 13 | ≈ 4.5 h |

## 6. Caveats the PI should weigh

1. **Deterministic runs break off-array neutrality for C and F.** The off-array share at n = 160 is 0.023 for C_det and
   0.048 for F_r16_det (base 0.19); H_det is fine at 0.18. Under temperature 0, a route-calibrated off-array gene maps
   to a quantile strictly below the on-array maximum, so it can never win the argmax. The randomised rule (the claim
   estimand) is unaffected. The DESIGN-required deterministic run for C and F is therefore biased against off-array
   genes. I recommend reporting it with this caveat, or fixing the calibration's top quantile before the freeze. A fix
   would be a frozen-code change and needs your decision.
2. **H outperforms the true-degree hub oracle** on dev1 (0.0498 vs 0.0420 at n = 160) and dev2 (0.0661 vs 0.0579).
   Its degree model ranks true query degree poorly (Spearman 0.02–0.21 along its trajectories, `degree_diagnostic.csv`).
   - H's gain is therefore not "finding true hubs".
   - The likely mechanism is that adaptive selectors pick genes whose array columns are SL-rich in the screened rows.
     Their rows then reveal many interactions in target columns, which the predictor legitimately uses as reciprocal
     and propensity information under the brief's observation model. This is consistent with the larger gain on
     on-array target rows and on E_VV.
   - Selection never sees D_val columns (SelectorView mask; poisoning tests pass), so this is not leakage, but the
     mechanism should be verified before the report claims it.
3. **Every knob and the ranking were chosen on the same 3 dev splits** (§8 as designed). Expect some shrinkage on T,
   as the λ screen illustrates.
